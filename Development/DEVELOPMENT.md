# Noxus Department — Development Documentation

> This documentation was compiled after the project's development based on the project's materials, development process, and team experience.

## 1. Project Overview

The Noxus Department project was created from a school seminar proposal about fungi, but was developed to go beyond a conventional presentation.

The team combined scientific research with a fictional narrative, creating a research organization called **Noxus Department** and using different media to construct its universe.

The project combines:

- Scientific content
- Script and narrative
- Visual identity
- Graphic materials
- 3D environments
- Audiovisual production
- Chroma key
- Web development
- 360° / VR

The objective was not only to tell a story, but to make the audience experience that story through different environments and media.

## 2. Project Structure

The project was organized into two layers that work together.

### Scientific Layer

The scientific layer presents real information about fungi and serves as the basis of the seminar.

### Narrative Layer

Based on the scientific content, the project introduces the fictional Noxus Department and a situation taking place in **2043**, involving an experimental organism and a containment failure.

These parts were not treated as separate projects. Scientific research provides the foundation for the experience, while the narrative transforms that content into a visual and immersive experience.

## 3. Scientific Research

The first stage was to gather the knowledge necessary for the seminar.

The research addressed topics including:

- Characteristics of fungi
- Ecology
- Feeding
- Reproduction
- Relationships with other organisms
- Environmental importance
- Applications
- Potential impacts and risks

The scientific research served as the starting point for the rest of the project.

## 4. Creation of Noxus Department

After defining the scientific proposal, the team created the fictional **Noxus Department**.

Within the project's universe, Noxus is an organization dedicated to botanical and experimental research.

### 4.1 Visual Identity

The visual identity was built around an:

- Institutional aesthetic
- Scientific aesthetic
- Corporate aesthetic
- Dark atmosphere
- Slightly unsettling tone
- Style that does not depend on exaggerated futuristic elements

The visual identity mainly uses black, dark green, white, and gray, with red used selectively for alerts and emergency situations.

The intention was for documents, posters, screens, environments, and the website to appear as if they belonged to the same organization.

## 5. Narrative Development — 2043

The narrative is set in **2043**.

The Noxus Department works with an experimental organism related to fungi and plants. As the narrative progresses, the situation changes from controlled research into a situation of loss of control.

The environments were used to communicate this progression without relying exclusively on verbal explanations.

The defined sequence was:

**Forest → City → Bedroom → News Broadcast → Laboratory**

## 6. Environment Development

### 6.1 Forest

The forest represents the beginning of the experience.

It is the natural environment and the first contact with the organism. The intention is to introduce the phenomenon before directly associating it with the Noxus Department.

### 6.2 City

The city represents the expansion of the phenomenon into an urban environment.

The change in setting increases the scale of the situation: what initially appeared to be restricted to a natural location begins to appear in spaces used by the public.

### 6.3 Bedroom

The bedroom reduces the scale of the experience again.

The phenomenon is presented in a personal and everyday space, bringing the situation closer to the viewer.

Together, these first three environments create a progression:

**natural → urban → personal**

### 6.4 News Broadcast

The news broadcast represents the moment when the situation stops being only an observed event and becomes a public issue.

A video was recorded with a person speaking in front of a chroma key. The background was removed, allowing the video to be integrated into the 3D environment as a news broadcast within the narrative.

The news broadcast connects the previous events with the final stage of the experience.

### 6.5 Abandoned Laboratory

The laboratory concept was changed during development.

Initially, it was intended mainly as an active scientific investigation environment. In the final version, it was changed into an **abandoned laboratory after the situation had gone out of control**.

The environment includes:

- Irregular lighting
- Flickering lights
- An abandoned appearance
- A strong light directed at the containment capsule
- The capsule previously used to contain the fungus

The capsule remains the main visual focus of the scene, but its containment system no longer works.

The laboratory therefore represents not only the research, but its consequence: the place created to control the organism failed to fulfill that function.

This change closes the narrative progression:

**discovery → expansion → proximity → disclosure → containment failure**

## 7. Fictional Organism

A fictional organism was visually developed for the Noxus Department universe.

Its appearance combines visual references from fungi, plants, and flowers, using organic and predominantly dark forms.

The organism appears throughout the environments to create visual and narrative continuity.

## 8. 3D Development

The environments were developed in **Blender**.

Each scene was planned according to its narrative function.

The development involved:

- Modeling
- Composition
- Lighting
- Materials
- Environmental objects
- Cameras
- Narrative elements
- Video integration
- Preparation for VR visualization

Each environment was intended to have its own identity while still belonging to the same universe.

## 9. Audiovisual Production

An important part of the project was the production of a video for the news broadcast.

A recording of approximately **5 minutes** was made using chroma key.

After recording, the background was removed so that the person could be integrated into the environment created in Blender.

The process involved:

- Recording
- Background removal
- Transparency processing
- Video processing
- Scene integration
- Playback tests
- Rendering tests

Tools such as **FFmpeg** were also used during processing.

When necessary, image sequences were used to preserve transparency during integration.

## 10. Blender Integration

The processed video was placed inside the news broadcast environment in Blender.

Tests were performed with:

- Transparency
- Materials
- Emission
- Lighting
- Composition
- Playback
- Rendering

Emission in the material allowed the video image to maintain its appearance without depending directly on the lighting of the environment.

## 11. 360° / VR Development

The experience was prepared for **360°/VR visualization**.

The intention was to allow the viewer to be inside the environments instead of simply observing a conventional image.

Tests involved:

- Panoramic camera
- Framing
- 360° environments
- VR visualization
- Device compatibility
- Integration of scene elements

VR was used as part of the presentation and as one of the main ways to experience the project.

## 12. Graphic Materials

To make the Noxus Department more convincing, materials were created to simulate the organization's internal and public communication.

These included:

- Posters
- Notices
- Documents
- Signs
- Emergency materials
- Institutional elements

The materials were designed to appear as part of the fictional company's universe rather than simply as decorative pieces of a school project.

## 13. Website Development

An institutional website for the Noxus Department was developed.

The objective was to extend the project beyond the presentation and create a digital extension of the fictional universe.

The website functions as part of the organization's identity and complements the physical and audiovisual materials.

**Official project website:**

- Test location

## 18. Decisions and Changes During Development

The project was not developed from a completely fixed idea.

Some decisions changed as production progressed.

The main change involved the laboratory concept.

The initial idea of an active scientific laboratory was replaced by an abandoned environment with irregular lighting, flickering lights, and a strong light highlighting the containment capsule.

This change was incorporated into the final result because it communicated the idea that the situation had gone beyond Noxus's ability to control it more effectively.

This type of change was part of the development process and reflects how the project was refined as the scenes, narrative, and technical resources were built.

## 19. Final Result

The final project combines:

**Science + Fiction + Narrative + Design + Audiovisual + 3D + Web + VR**

The experience begins with a real scientific subject and gradually builds a fictional universe around it.

The progression through the environments allows the narrative to be perceived visually:

- **Forest** — origin
- **City** — expansion
- **Bedroom** — proximity
- **News Broadcast** — public exposure
- **Laboratory** — consequence and containment failure

The website, documents, posters, and other materials expand the universe beyond the presentation.

The abandoned laboratory serves as the final image of this trajectory: the capsule created to contain the organism is still present, but its function has been lost.

## 21. Summary

Noxus Department was developed as a multidisciplinary project in which different areas were used to construct a single experience.

Scientific research provides the starting point. The narrative gives context. Design establishes the identity. 3D creates the environments. Audiovisual production gives movement to the story. The website expands the universe. And virtual reality places the viewer inside it.

The project is therefore neither only a seminar about fungi nor only a fictional story: it is the integration of both into an experience built by a team of six students.
